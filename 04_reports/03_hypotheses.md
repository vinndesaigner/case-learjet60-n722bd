# 03 — Preliminary Hypotheses

> ⚠️ INDEPENDENT OSINT INVESTIGATION — Not official.
> These are hypotheses, **not conclusions**.

## H1 — In-Flight Icing leading to Aerodynamic Stall
- **Claim:** The pilot reported "light clear ice" before the rapid descent. Accumulation of ice on the airframe likely disrupted airflow, causing a sudden loss of lift or a tail-plane stall, leading to the uncontrolled dive.
- **Supporting evidence:** `[EVID-03]` (ATC ice report), `[EVID-01]` (Sudden loss of altitude without prior engine failure indications).
- **Counter-evidence:** Pilot reported "light" ice, which usually isn't catastrophic. Learjets are certified for known icing conditions.
- **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Falsifiable by:** FDR data showing control inputs vs. aerodynamic forces, or NTSB wreckage analysis of the tail section.

## H2 — Spatial Disorientation / Pilot Error
- **Claim:** Encountering turbulence and ice at night/in IMC (Instrument Meteorological Conditions) led to spatial disorientation, causing the pilot to pull the wrong control or lose control of the aircraft.
- **Supporting evidence:** `[EVID-03]` ("We are overspeed" indicates the pilot was aware of the speed, but perhaps couldn't correct the pitch).
- **Confidence:** Low.
- **Falsifiable by:** CVR (Cockpit Voice Recorder) audio, which would reveal alarms, panic, or erroneous instrument readings.

## H3 — Mechanical Failure (e.g., Flight Control Jam)
- **Claim:** A sudden mechanical failure (like a trim runaway or flight control surface jam) forced the aircraft into a dive that the pilots could not physically counteract.
- **Supporting evidence:** `[EVID-01]` (Extreme descent rate of 29,000 fpm suggests the aircraft was not just stalling, but actively being pushed down).
- **Confidence:** Low.
- **Falsifiable by:** Maintenance records (if available) and wreckage analysis.
